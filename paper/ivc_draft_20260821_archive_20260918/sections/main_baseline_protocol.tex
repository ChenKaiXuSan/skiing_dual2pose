\paragraph{Expanded pose-only baselines.}
The direct-input groups in Tables~\ref{tab:native_reference} and~\ref{tab:ski_poseptz_reference} include both single views, equal canonical averaging, sequence-level right-to-left similarity alignment followed by equal averaging, and a 3D quality-weighted average. The latter assigns one weight to each view and sequence using temporal second-difference magnitude and temporal bone-length inconsistency, each normalized by median bone length; it uses neither detection confidence nor reference poses. A collapsed skeleton is rejected when the other view is non-degenerate. These are controlled geometric and heuristic baselines rather than published learned fusion systems.

We additionally train a framewise cross-view MLP, a temporal convolutional fusion baseline, and canonical averaging followed by SmoothNet~\cite{zeng2022smoothnet}. The MLP concatenates both complete poses and predicts an additive residual through two 256-unit ReLU layers with dropout 0.1. The TCN uses concatenated pose channels, two 128-channel hidden convolutions, and an output convolution; all three kernels have width 5, with dropout 0.1 in the hidden layers. SmoothNet retains the official 512-unit encoder, five 512--128--512 residual blocks, dropout 0.25, and temporal decoder, with its input and output windows adapted to the same 30 sampled frames. It refines the average pose with shared temporal weights across joint coordinates. The MLP and TCN are local controlled architectures; SmoothNet is an adaptation of the published temporal refinement method, not a competing multi-view reconstruction network.

\paragraph{Published-method adaptations.}
Three external methods provide related comparisons under explicitly different input and task contracts. STRIDE~\cite{lal2025stride} refines the complete canonical-average 3D sequence using its released model and is evaluated as an adapted temporal post-processor. DeciWatch~\cite{zeng2022deciwatch} follows the closest official 31-token configuration: archived frame 29 is repeated once, tokens 0, 10, 20, and 30 are observed, and the recovered output is cropped back to the original 30 frames. Thus, four unique source frames are used per window rather than describing the adaptation as the original 10\%/101-frame setting. The common-13 model is trained from scratch because no compatible released checkpoint is available; Unity selects the minimum validation loss and Ski-PTZ-Pose uses the fixed final epoch. MetaPose~\cite{usman2022metapose} uses the native monocular 3D estimates together with synchronized estimated 2D joints, replaces detector heat-map mixtures by uncertainty fitted only on training residuals, and trains its common-13 stages without 3D supervision. Unity selects stages by validation 2D error, whereas Ski-PTZ-Pose uses fixed final epochs. These are domain adaptations rather than claims of reproducing the published benchmark numbers.

\paragraph{Additional-input geometric references.}
A separate group uses the native SAM3D estimated 2D joints and supplied camera calibration to triangulate corresponding observations by DLT. The robust variant rejects joint-frame estimates whose maximum two-view reprojection residual exceeds 25 pixels and fills gaps by temporal interpolation of accepted estimates, with nearest-value extension at sequence boundaries. Tracks with no accepted point retain their finite ungated DLT estimates; validity and fallback counts are archived. Every fixed test sample is retained. The DLT-residual MLP predicts a correction from the complete canonicalized DLT pose, log-scaled reprojection residuals, and validity indicators, using two 256-unit hidden layers. No annotation coordinates are supplied as predictive features. All three methods are evaluated after independently applying the same body-coordinate transformation to their predictions and to the reference. Ski-PTZ-Pose annotations are pelvis-relative in every frame: the source hip joint and the midpoint of the two hip joints are zero. We therefore first subtract each geometric prediction's own per-frame hip midpoint (common-13 joints 4 and 5) from its joints, removing world translation without using reference coordinates; Unity retains its world-coordinate trajectory. The subsequent batch-referenced body transform is unchanged. The prediction preprocessing fits no scale or additional similarity transform to test annotations. The separately reported PA-MPJPE uses an evaluation-only reference alignment for every method, as defined in the metrics subsection. This group therefore has additional input requirements and does not establish a common-input ranking against the pose-only methods.

\paragraph{Baseline training and selection.}
All added learned baselines use seed 42, AdamW with learning rate $10^{-3}$ and weight decay 0.01, and a fixed 100-epoch budget. The residual MLP and TCN objectives combine position L1 error with weight 0.01 on predicted second-difference magnitude. SmoothNet uses its position L1 objective plus weight 0.1 on acceleration L1 error. Unity uses the complete action-disjoint training and validation indices, batch size 4096 for training and validation, and the checkpoint with lowest validation MPJPE on all 15 joints; evaluation uses batch size 256. SmoothNet accumulates gradients over microbatches of 2048 after the complete 4096-sequence batch has been canonicalized, preserving the batch anchor. The archived Unity CanonFuse3D \texttt{last.ckpt} has weights identical to the stored epoch-89 validation-selected checkpoint.

The Ski training index contains 150 entries, of which the original loader admits 145: five pairs involving camera 0 in subject 2, sequence 202 have no surviving shared frames. All added Ski models use these same 145 eligible training entries, batch size 4, and the fixed final epoch-99 checkpoint; no test metric selects their parameters or stopping point. Training follows the archived shuffled, complete-batch policy, processing 192,512 Unity or 144 Ski entries per epoch, with the omitted entries changing with the seeded shuffle. Validation and test retain their final partial batches. CanonFuse3D retains its predeclared dataset-specific checkpoint, so these are single-run comparisons under stated training budgets, not averages over independent training seeds.
